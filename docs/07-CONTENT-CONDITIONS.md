# `/conditions/weight-loss`

**Target:** `dietitian for weight loss`, `weight loss diet plan India`, `how to lose weight without giving up rice`
**Meta title:** `Weight Loss Diet Plan — Personalised Nutrition | Ozmo Diet Clinic`
**Meta description:** `A weight loss plan built around Indian food and your actual routine — with tracking, fortnightly follow-ups and a dietitian who adjusts it when it stops working.`

**H1:** Weight loss, without the part where you quit in week three
**Sub:** You don't need another list of banned foods. You need a plan that fits your kitchen and someone who notices when it stops working.

## Is this you?
- You've lost weight before and put it all back
- You eat "reasonably healthy" and the weight still isn't moving
- You're stuck at the same number no matter what you try
- Diet plans you've been given required food you don't like or can't cook
- You lose motivation somewhere around week three
- You've been told to "just eat less and move more" and it hasn't worked

If you recognised three or more of these, the problem is almost certainly the *approach*, not you.

## Why weight loss usually fails
It's rarely willpower. In practice it comes down to a handful of predictable things:

**The plan didn't fit the kitchen.** If your plan needs separate cooking and your household cooks one meal, the plan loses.

**It was too aggressive to sustain.** Very low calorie plans work brilliantly for eleven days and then collapse — usually taking some muscle with them, which makes the next attempt harder.

**Nothing accounted for real life.** Weddings, travel, festivals, office food, night shifts. A plan that only works on a perfect Tuesday isn't a plan.

**There was no feedback loop.** You couldn't see whether it was working until the scale moved, and by the time it didn't, you'd already given up.

**Nobody adjusted it.** Bodies adapt. Plateaus are normal. A plan that never changes will stop working — that's physiology, not failure.

## How nutrition actually helps
Sustainable fat loss comes from a moderate, consistent energy deficit — with enough protein to protect muscle, enough fibre to keep you full, and a structure you can hold for months rather than weeks.

The levers that do most of the work in Indian diets, in rough order of impact:
1. **Portion of the staple** — rice and roti quantity, not elimination
2. **Protein at every meal** — most Indian vegetarian diets are meaningfully short here
3. **Meal timing** — especially the gap between lunch and dinner, and how late dinner is
4. **The invisible calories** — oil, sugar in tea, evening snacking, weekend eating out
5. **Fibre and volume** — salad and vegetables before the carbohydrate portion
6. **Daily movement** — walking, not necessarily the gym

## What Ozmo does about it
**We start from your current diet.** Your first plan will look familiar — corrected, not replaced. Changes you barely notice are changes you'll still be making in month three.
**We set a rate your body can hold.** Steady and unglamorous beats fast and rebounding.
**We track more than weight.** Waist, hip, energy, adherence. Weight lies for weeks at a time; measurements don't.
**We adjust every fortnight.** When the plan stops working, we change the plan. That's the whole job.

## Foods that usually help
Dal, rajma, chana and legumes · curd and paneer · eggs, chicken, fish · green leafy vegetables · seasonal fruit (whole, not juiced) · whole grains — jowar, bajra, oats, brown rice · nuts and seeds in measured portions · plenty of water

## Foods usually worth limiting
Sugary drinks and packaged juice · deep-fried snacks · refined flour (maida) items · sweets outside occasions · excess oil and ghee (limited, not banned) · very late heavy dinners · "healthy" packaged food that is mostly sugar

> **Note:** limiting is not banning. A plan with zero enjoyable food in it has a shelf life of about two weeks.

## A sample day
> *An example only. Yours will be built around your food, routine and health.*

| Time | Meal |
|---|---|
| 7:00 AM | Warm water · 5 soaked almonds |
| 8:30 AM | Vegetable poha *or* 2 besan chilla *or* oats with milk and fruit |
| 11:00 AM | Seasonal fruit *or* buttermilk |
| 1:30 PM | Salad first · 2 roti · dal · sabzi · curd |
| 5:00 PM | Roasted chana *or* sprouts *or* tea with 2 biscuits |
| 8:00 PM | 1 roti *or* small rice portion · paneer/chicken/dal · sabzi · salad |

## What the journey usually looks like
**Weeks 1–2** — Getting used to timing and portions. Early movement on the scale, some of it water. Logging becomes a habit.
**Weeks 3–6** — The real work. Steady loss. This is where most people historically quit — and where fortnightly follow-ups make the difference.
**Weeks 7–12** — Adjustments as your body adapts. A plateau somewhere here is normal and expected. Clothes usually change before the scale does.
**Month 4 onwards** — Consolidation. Learning to hold the result through social meals, travel and festivals.

## Common myths
**"Carbs make you fat."** Excess calories do. Rice and roti in the right quantity are in almost every plan we write.
**"Eating after 7pm makes you gain weight."** The clock isn't magic. Late *heavy* dinners are the problem — and they're usually a symptom of skipping meals earlier.
**"You must do cardio to lose weight."** Nutrition drives fat loss. Movement supports it, helps hold it, and does a great deal for your health — but you won't out-run a poor diet.
**"Detox drinks / green tea burn fat."** They don't. Anything sold on that promise is selling you something.
**"Skipping meals speeds it up."** It usually leads to a larger, less controlled meal later. Consistency beats restriction.

## FAQs
**How much weight can I lose in a month?** It depends on your starting weight, health, medication and consistency — and anyone quoting a number before meeting you is guessing. We'll give you a realistic range after your consultation.
**Do I have to stop eating rice?** No. We adjust the portion and what it's eaten with.
**Will I need to cook separately from my family?** No. That's usually the fastest route to failure, so we build around the household meal.
**Do I need a gym membership?** No. Walking is enough to start. If you train already, we build around it.
**What if I have thyroid or PCOS?** Then a standard weight-loss plan probably won't work well, and you'd be better served by our `Hormonal Balance programme →`.
**What if I hit a plateau?** Expected. That's what the fortnightly reviews are for.
**Is this safe if I'm on medication?** Tell us exactly what you take. We build around it and never advise changing it.

## When to see a doctor first
Unexplained weight loss you didn't intend · symptoms of an eating disorder · sudden or unexplained weight gain · pregnancy. In these cases, see your doctor first — we'll say the same thing.

# `/conditions/diabetes`

**Target:** `diet plan for diabetes India`, `diabetic diet chart Indian`, `dietitian for blood sugar`
**Meta title:** `Diabetes Diet Plan — Nutrition Support for Blood Sugar | Ozmo Diet Clinic`
**Meta description:** `Nutrition guidance for type 2 diabetes and pre-diabetes, built around your reports, your medication and Indian home food. Dietitian-led, with regular follow-ups.`

**H1:** Eating well while managing your blood sugar
**Sub:** Not a list of everything you can't have. A plan built around your reports, your medication and the food your family actually cooks.

## Is this you?
- You've been told your fasting sugar or HbA1c needs attention
- You have pre-diabetes and want to act before it progresses
- You're managing type 2 diabetes and your diet advice was a photocopied list
- You've been told "no rice, no sugar, no potato" and no one told you what to actually eat
- Your readings swing and you can't see the pattern
- Family history and you want to get ahead of it

## Why blood sugar becomes difficult to manage
In simple terms, type 2 diabetes involves the body becoming less responsive to insulin, and insulin production not keeping up with demand. Blood glucose then stays higher than it should.

What you eat affects this directly — but so does *when* you eat, *what you eat it with*, how much you move, how well you sleep, and your medication. Which is why a food list alone rarely works.

## How nutrition helps
Nutrition is a recognised, central part of managing type 2 diabetes, and it works through mechanisms that are well understood:

**Carbohydrate quantity and quality.** Not elimination — distribution. Spreading carbohydrate across the day rather than loading it into one or two meals tends to produce steadier readings.
**Pairing.** Carbohydrate eaten with protein, fat and fibre is absorbed more gradually than carbohydrate eaten alone. A roti with dal and salad behaves differently from a roti alone.
**Meal timing and spacing.** Long gaps followed by large meals are one of the most common causes of erratic readings.
**Fibre.** Whole grains, legumes, vegetables and whole fruit.
**Weight and waist.** Where relevant, even modest reductions can improve how the body handles glucose.
**Movement after meals.** A ten-minute walk after lunch and dinner is one of the highest-value, lowest-effort habits available.

> We can't and won't tell you nutrition will cure or reverse your condition. What we can say is that it's a well-established part of managing it, and that your numbers may improve — with any conclusions about your condition or medication coming from your doctor.

## What Ozmo does about it
**We read your reports first.** HbA1c, fasting and post-prandial readings, lipid profile, kidney function if available. The plan starts from your numbers.
**We build around your medication.** Timing food relative to medication matters, and we go through it with you.
**We give you meals, not bans.** A plan with actual food in it. Rice appears in most of our diabetic plans — in a measured portion, paired properly, at the right time.
**We watch the pattern.** You log meals and readings; we look for what's driving the spikes. Usually it's two or three specific habits, and they're fixable.

## Foods that usually help
Whole grains — jowar, bajra, ragi, oats, brown rice in measured portions · dal, chana, rajma, sprouts · green leafy and non-starchy vegetables · curd and paneer · eggs, fish, chicken · nuts and seeds, measured · whole fruit — guava, apple, papaya, pear, berries · methi, karela and bitter vegetables as part of a balanced plate · plenty of water

## Foods usually worth limiting
Sugary drinks, packaged juice, sweetened lassi · sweets and mithai · maida — white bread, biscuits, bakery items · deep-fried snacks · very large single portions of rice or potato · fruit juice even when fresh · alcohol `[discuss with your doctor]`

## A sample day
> *An example only. Yours depends on your reports, medication and preferences.*

| Time | Meal |
|---|---|
| 7:00 AM | Warm water · 5 soaked almonds |
| 8:30 AM | Vegetable oats *or* moong dal chilla with curd *or* 2 multigrain roti with sabzi |
| 11:00 AM | Guava, apple or pear *or* buttermilk |
| 1:30 PM | Salad first · 2 multigrain roti · dal · green sabzi · curd |
| 5:00 PM | Roasted chana *or* sprouts chaat · green tea |
| 8:00 PM | 1–2 roti *or* measured rice · paneer/chicken/dal · sabzi · salad |
| Habits | Dinner by 8:30 PM · 10-minute walk after lunch and dinner · consistent meal timing |

## Common myths
**"Diabetics can't eat rice."** Portion, pairing and timing matter far more than elimination. Most of our plans include rice.
**"Sugar-free means safe."** Many sugar-free products are refined flour and fat. Read what's actually in it.
**"Fruit is bad for diabetics."** Whole fruit in sensible portions is generally fine and useful. Fruit *juice* is a different thing entirely.
**"Karela juice / methi water will fix it."** They can be part of a good diet. They are not a treatment, and they will not replace your medication.
**"If my sugar is normal now, I can stop."** Normal readings usually mean the current approach is working — which is an argument for continuing it, not stopping.

## FAQs
**Can diet reverse diabetes?** We won't claim that. Nutrition and lifestyle are a central part of management, and many people see improvement in their numbers. Any conclusion about your condition, and any change to medication, is your doctor's decision.
**Do I need to stop sugar completely?** Added sugar gets managed carefully. The plan is built to be sustainable, not absolute.
**Will you adjust my medication?** Never. That's your doctor's job, and we'll say so every time it comes up.
**How often should I test?** Follow your doctor's advice. Whatever readings you take, log them — they make your plan sharper.
**What about fasting — Karva Chauth, Navratri, Ramadan?** Plannable, and worth discussing with your doctor too. Tell us in advance and we'll build for it.
**I'm pre-diabetic. Is this worth doing?** This is the best possible time to act, and the point at which nutrition has the most leverage.
**Can I still eat out?** Yes. We'll go through how to order.

## When to see a doctor first
Very high or very low readings · symptoms like excessive thirst, frequent urination, blurred vision, unexplained weight loss · any change in medication · type 1 diabetes management · signs of complications. Nutrition support sits alongside medical care, never instead of it.

# `/conditions/pcos`

**Target:** `PCOS diet plan India`, `PCOD diet chart`, `dietitian for PCOS`
**Meta title:** `PCOS & PCOD Diet Plan — Nutrition Support | Ozmo Diet Clinic`
**Meta description:** `Nutrition guidance for PCOS and PCOD built around insulin sensitivity, symptoms and Indian food — with symptom tracking and regular dietitian follow-ups.`

**H1:** PCOS: when "just lose weight" isn't useful advice
**Sub:** If standard diet plans haven't worked for you, that isn't a discipline problem. PCOS changes what the body does with food — so the plan has to change too.

## Is this you?
- Diagnosed with PCOS or PCOD, and told to lose weight with no further guidance
- Irregular or missing periods
- Weight that climbs easily and comes off slowly
- Constant cravings, especially for sugar and carbohydrate
- Fatigue, low energy, poor sleep
- Acne, hair thinning, or unwanted hair growth
- You're doing what worked for your friends and it isn't working for you

## Why PCOS makes this harder
PCOS is a hormonal condition, and for a large proportion of people who have it, insulin resistance is part of the picture — the body produces more insulin to do the same job. Higher circulating insulin makes fat storage easier, fat loss harder, and cravings stronger, and it interacts with the hormonal cycle in ways that affect symptoms.

Which means the standard advice — eat less, move more — is working against a stacked deck. Not impossible. Just different.

## How nutrition helps
Nutrition and lifestyle are a well-recognised part of managing PCOS. The approach that tends to matter most:

**Carbohydrate quality and pairing.** Not low-carb — *better* carb. Whole grains, legumes, and always paired with protein and fibre.
**Steady meal timing.** Long gaps drive the crash-and-crave cycle that makes PCOS eating so hard to control.
**Protein at every meal.** Helps satiety, helps cravings, helps preserve muscle during fat loss.
**Enough fibre.** Supports fullness, digestion, and steadier energy.
**Movement — especially resistance work.** Muscle improves how the body handles glucose.
**Sleep and stress.** Both directly affect the hormonal picture. Ignoring them makes the food work harder than it should.
**Specific nutrients.** Vitamin D, B12, iron and others are commonly low. Worth testing, and worth addressing.

## What Ozmo does about it
**We take the condition seriously.** No "just eat less." The plan is built for a body that handles carbohydrate differently.
**We work on insulin sensitivity first.** Carb quality, pairing, distribution and timing — before we touch calories aggressively.
**We track symptoms, not just weight.** Energy, cravings, sleep, cycle regularity, skin and hair. These often improve before the scale does, and seeing that keeps people going.
**We plan for the long term.** PCOS is managed across years. The plan has to be one you can live inside indefinitely.

## Foods that usually help
Dal, chana, rajma, sprouts · paneer, curd, eggs, chicken, fish · whole grains — jowar, bajra, ragi, oats · green leafy vegetables · flax, chia, pumpkin seeds · nuts in measured portions · berries, guava, apple, pear · cinnamon and methi as part of normal cooking · plenty of water

## Foods usually worth limiting
Sugary drinks and desserts · maida — bread, biscuits, bakery, instant noodles · deep-fried snacks · large portions of refined carbohydrate alone · packaged "diet" foods high in sugar · excessive caffeine if sleep is affected

> **On the internet's favourite PCOS bans:** blanket gluten-free and dairy-free are not necessary for most people with PCOS. If there's a specific reason in your case, we'll discuss it. We don't remove entire food groups on trend.

## A sample day
> *An example only.*

| Time | Meal |
|---|---|
| 7:00 AM | Warm water · 5 soaked almonds and 2 walnuts |
| 8:30 AM | Moong dal chilla with curd *or* vegetable oats *or* 2 eggs with multigrain toast |
| 11:00 AM | Guava or apple · a few pumpkin seeds |
| 1:30 PM | Salad first · 2 jowar/multigrain roti · dal · green sabzi · curd |
| 5:00 PM | Roasted chana *or* sprouts chaat *or* buttermilk · green tea |
| 8:00 PM | 1–2 roti *or* measured brown rice · paneer/chicken/dal · sabzi · salad |
| Habits | Consistent timing · 30 min walking · resistance training 2–3×/week · sleep before 11 PM |

## Common myths
**"You can't lose weight with PCOS."** Slower, usually. Not impossible. The method has to be right.
**"Everyone with PCOS must go gluten-free and dairy-free."** Not supported for most people. Unnecessary elimination makes eating harder and adherence worse.
**"PCOS can be cured with diet."** PCOS is managed, not cured. Symptoms often respond well — which is worth a great deal, and is a different claim.
**"You need to eat very low carb."** Quality and pairing generally matter more than severe restriction, and severe restriction is rarely sustainable.
**"Losing weight will fix everything."** It often helps significantly. It isn't the whole picture, and lean people have PCOS too.

## FAQs
**Will my periods become regular?** Many people find cycle regularity improves alongside nutrition and lifestyle changes. We can't promise it, and it's something to track with your gynaecologist.
**Do I need to be overweight to have PCOS?** No. Lean PCOS is real, and the nutrition approach still centres on insulin sensitivity and symptom management.
**Should I take inositol / supplements?** Discuss with your doctor. Where a supplement makes sense we'll explain the reasoning — and we don't sell any, so there's no incentive to over-recommend.
**Can this help with fertility?** Nutrition and lifestyle are frequently part of a broader fertility plan, but that plan belongs with your treating doctor. We work alongside them.
**How long until I notice something?** Energy and cravings often shift first, sometimes within weeks. Weight and cycle changes usually take longer.
**Is PCOS the same as PCOD?** The terms are used interchangeably in everyday conversation in India. Your doctor's diagnosis is what matters; the nutrition approach is broadly similar.

## When to see a doctor first
Diagnosis · fertility planning · any medication decisions · severe or worsening symptoms. We provide nutrition support alongside that care.

# `/conditions/thyroid`

**Target:** `thyroid diet plan India`, `hypothyroidism diet chart`, `dietitian for thyroid weight gain`
**Meta title:** `Thyroid Diet Plan — Nutrition Support | Ozmo Diet Clinic`
**Meta description:** `Nutrition guidance for hypothyroidism and hyperthyroidism — meal timing around medication, nutrient adequacy, and realistic weight management. Dietitian-led.`

**H1:** Thyroid, weight, and what food can actually do
**Sub:** No food fixes a thyroid condition. What nutrition can do is support your energy, your weight and how you feel day to day — and that's worth doing properly.

## Is this you?
- Diagnosed hypothyroidism or hyperthyroidism, on medication
- Weight gain that started around the time of diagnosis
- Constant fatigue no matter how much you sleep
- Hair fall, dry skin, feeling cold, low mood
- Your reports are "normal" now, but you still don't feel right
- You've been told to avoid cabbage, soya and half the vegetable market

## What's actually happening
The thyroid produces hormones that regulate metabolism. In hypothyroidism, there's not enough — metabolism slows, energy drops, weight becomes easier to gain and harder to lose. In hyperthyroidism, the reverse.

Medication addresses the hormone level. It doesn't automatically fix the weight, the energy or the eating patterns that built up while you felt exhausted — and that's the space nutrition works in.

## How nutrition helps
**Let's be honest first:** no food, spice, juice or supplement treats a thyroid condition. Your medication does that. Anything claiming otherwise is not being straight with you.

What nutrition genuinely does:
**Timing around medication.** Thyroid medication is generally taken on an empty stomach, and certain foods and supplements — notably calcium and iron — interfere with absorption if taken too close to it. Getting this right matters, and many people don't know it.
**Nutrient adequacy.** Iodine, selenium, zinc, iron, vitamin D and B12 all matter to thyroid function and to how you feel. Deficiencies are common and worth testing.
**Realistic weight management.** A slower metabolism means a smaller margin for error and a slower rate of loss. Achievable — with consistency and correct expectations.
**Energy and satiety.** Protein, fibre and steady meal timing help with the fatigue and the appetite swings.
**Gut and digestion.** Constipation is a common companion of hypothyroidism, and it's very treatable with fibre, fluid and movement.

## What Ozmo does about it
**We set expectations honestly.** Weight loss with a thyroid condition is usually slower. We'd rather tell you that at the start than have you conclude you've failed at week six.
**We fix the medication-food timing.** Simple, frequently wrong, and immediately worth correcting.
**We look for nutrient gaps.** Share your reports and we'll build accordingly, flagging anything worth asking your doctor about.
**We build a plan you can hold for years.** Thyroid conditions are usually lifelong. Short-term plans miss the point.

## Foods that usually help
Iodised salt in normal cooking · fish and seafood · eggs, curd, paneer · dal and legumes · nuts and seeds — especially brazil nuts and pumpkin seeds for selenium and zinc · whole grains · plenty of vegetables and fibre · adequate protein at every meal · plenty of water

## Foods usually worth limiting
Highly processed and fried foods · excess sugar · very large quantities of raw cruciferous vegetables *(cooked, in normal amounts, they're fine — see myths)* · alcohol in excess · calcium and iron supplements taken close to thyroid medication

## A sample day
> *An example only. Note the medication gap — this is the detail most people get wrong.*

| Time | Meal |
|---|---|
| 6:30 AM | Thyroid medication on an empty stomach · water only |
| 7:30 AM | *(≥60 min after medication)* Tea/coffee if you have it |
| 8:30 AM | 2 besan chilla *or* vegetable oats *or* 2 eggs with multigrain toast |
| 11:00 AM | Fruit · a few pumpkin seeds |
| 1:30 PM | Salad · 2 roti · dal · sabzi · curd |
| 5:00 PM | Roasted chana *or* sprouts · green tea |
| 8:00 PM | 1–2 roti *or* measured rice · paneer/fish/chicken/dal · sabzi · salad |
| Note | Keep calcium or iron supplements at least 4 hours away from thyroid medication |

## Common myths
**"You must avoid cabbage, cauliflower, broccoli and soya entirely."** The most over-stated advice in this area. In normal, cooked quantities these are fine for most people. Extremely large raw amounts are a different conversation — and almost nobody eats that way.
**"Thyroid means you can never lose weight."** Slower, yes. Never, no.
**"Coriander water / specific juices cure thyroid."** They don't. Please keep taking your medication.
**"If TSH is normal, diet doesn't matter."** Normal TSH is the medication working. Your energy, weight and nutrient status are still worth attention.
**"Gluten-free is essential for everyone with thyroid issues."** Relevant for people who also have coeliac disease. Not a blanket rule.

## FAQs
**Will I lose weight if my thyroid is controlled?** Usually yes, with a consistent plan — often more slowly than someone without the condition.
**How long should I wait after medication before eating?** Commonly 30–60 minutes, but follow your doctor's instruction — it varies with the medication.
**Can I drink tea or coffee in the morning?** Not immediately after your medication. Leave a gap.
**Should I take iodine supplements?** Not without your doctor's advice. Too much iodine can be as much of a problem as too little.
**Do I need to test anything before starting?** A recent thyroid panel helps a great deal, plus vitamin D, B12 and iron if you have them.
**Hyperthyroidism — is the approach different?** Yes, meaningfully. The focus shifts to adequate energy intake, nutrient repletion and often weight maintenance rather than loss.

## When to see a doctor first
Diagnosis and medication · symptoms that worsen · pregnancy with a thyroid condition · nodules or swelling. Nutrition support runs alongside your endocrinologist's or physician's care.

# `/conditions/cholesterol`

**Target:** `diet for high cholesterol India`, `heart healthy Indian diet`
**Meta title:** `High Cholesterol Diet Plan — Heart-Healthy Nutrition | Ozmo Diet Clinic`
**Meta description:** `Nutrition guidance for high cholesterol and heart health, built around Indian food. Practical changes to fats, fibre and cooking — not a bland diet.`

**H1:** Heart-healthy eating that isn't boring food
**Sub:** High cholesterol usually responds well to specific, targeted changes — most of which are about *how* you cook, not giving up everything you enjoy.

## Is this you?
- Recent lipid profile flagged high LDL or triglycerides, or low HDL
- Your doctor said "watch your diet" and left it there
- Family history of heart disease or high cholesterol
- You've been told to stop eating ghee, oil and eggs entirely
- You're on statins and want your diet working with them

## What the numbers mean *(in plain language)*
Your lipid profile typically reports total cholesterol, LDL, HDL and triglycerides. Broadly: LDL contributes to plaque build-up in arteries; HDL helps clear cholesterol; triglycerides are blood fats that rise with excess calories, refined carbohydrate, sugar and alcohol.

**What your numbers mean for you specifically is a conversation for your doctor.** What we do is build the eating pattern around them.

## How nutrition helps
**Fat quality over fat quantity.** Replacing saturated and trans fats with unsaturated fats is one of the better-established levers. That means cooking oil choices, less deep-frying, fewer bakery and packaged items — not zero fat.
**Soluble fibre.** Oats, barley, legumes, apples, flaxseed. Genuinely useful and easy to add.
**Triglycerides respond to sugar and refined carbohydrate.** Often more than to dietary fat. This surprises people.
**Trans fats out.** Vanaspita, repeatedly reheated frying oil, many commercial bakery items. This is one of the few near-absolute recommendations we make.
**Weight and waist, where relevant.** Even modest reductions often show up in the lipid panel.
**Movement.** Regular activity tends to help HDL and triglycerides.

## What Ozmo does about it
**We start with the kitchen, not the plate.** Which oil, how much per person per month, how often things are deep-fried, whether frying oil is reused. These changes affect the whole household and cost nothing.
**We add before we remove.** Soluble fibre, nuts, and better fats go in first. Easier to sustain, and it works.
**We separate the LDL problem from the triglyceride problem.** They respond to different things, and treating them the same is why generic advice underperforms.
**We keep it edible.** A heart-healthy Indian diet is not boiled vegetables. Your food should still taste like food.

## Foods that usually help
Oats, barley, whole grains · dal, rajma, chana · almonds, walnuts, flax and chia · mustard, groundnut, rice bran or olive oil in measured quantities · fatty fish · plenty of vegetables and fruit · garlic, methi and turmeric in normal cooking · curd and low-fat milk

## Foods usually worth limiting
Deep-fried snacks · vanaspati and repeatedly reheated oil · bakery items, biscuits, packaged namkeen · red and processed meat · full-fat dairy in excess · sweets and sugary drinks *(especially for triglycerides)* · alcohol *(especially for triglycerides)*

## Common myths
**"Eggs cause high cholesterol."** For most people, dietary cholesterol from eggs has a modest effect on blood cholesterol. What matters more is what the eggs are cooked in and what they're eaten with.
**"Ghee is either poison or a superfood."** Neither. Quantity is the whole argument.
**"Only overweight people get high cholesterol."** Not true. Lean people can have a poor lipid profile — genetics matter.

## Why this is hard to self-diagnose
Symptoms often appear hours after the trigger. Multiple foods are eaten together. Stress, sleep, meal timing, hydration and eating speed all contribute. So the pattern is genuinely difficult to see from memory — which is why elimination-by-guesswork usually ends with fewer foods and the same symptoms.

## How nutrition helps
**Systematic logging first.** Food, timing, symptoms, severity — for two to three weeks. The dashboard makes it a few taps a day. Patterns emerge that memory never would have found.
**Fix the fundamentals before eliminating anything.** Fibre type and amount, water, meal timing, portion size, eating speed, the gap before lying down. A meaningful share of cases resolve here, without removing a single food.
**Targeted, temporary elimination — only if needed.** Specific, time-boxed, and *systematically reintroduced*. Permanent open-ended elimination is how people end up eating twelve foods.
**Rebuild variety.** More tolerated foods is the goal. A narrow diet isn't a healthy gut.

## What Ozmo does about it
**We ask you to log properly for two to three weeks.** It's the highest-value thing you can do, and it's the thing nobody has asked you to do systematically before.
**We look at habits, not just food.** How fast you eat, how late you eat, how long you sit after dinner, water timing, tea and coffee patterns.
**We keep eliminations narrow and temporary.** With a reintroduction plan written down before we start.
**We refer when we should.** Some symptoms need investigation, not a diet plan. We'll say so directly.

## Foods that usually help
Adequate soluble fibre — oats, psyllium, soaked chia · curd and fermented foods where tolerated · well-cooked vegetables · bananas, papaya, cooked apple · jeera, saunf, ajwain in cooking · plenty of water · smaller, more regular meals

## Foods often worth reviewing
Very spicy or heavily fried food · large late dinners · excess tea, coffee, carbonated drinks · alcohol · very large raw salads *(surprising to some — hard to digest for some people)* · dairy *(for some — worth testing properly, not assuming)* · artificial sweeteners, especially sugar alcohols

## Common myths
**"Curd is bad at night."** For most people it's perfectly fine. If it doesn't suit you specifically, that's individual — not a rule.
**"Everyone should eat more raw salad."** Raw fibre is hard work for some digestive systems. Cooked vegetables are sometimes the better answer.
**"Food intolerance blood tests will tell me my triggers."** Most commercially marketed intolerance panels aren't reliable. Systematic elimination and reintroduction tells you far more.
**"Bloating means I need a detox."** No. It usually means a fixable pattern in what, when or how you're eating.

## FAQs
**How long before I feel better?** Some people notice change within two weeks; some take longer. We'll be honest about what we're seeing.
**Do I have to give up dairy or wheat forever?** Almost certainly not. Tolerance is usually about quantity and context.
**Can you diagnose IBS?** No. We provide nutrition support alongside your doctor's diagnosis and care.
**Is a low-FODMAP diet right for me?** Sometimes — and it's a structured, time-limited protocol with a reintroduction phase, not a permanent diet. We'd only run it if it's genuinely indicated.

## When to see a doctor first — please read
See a doctor before starting any diet approach if you have: blood in your stool · unexplained weight loss · persistent vomiting · difficulty swallowing · severe or worsening pain · symptoms that began suddenly after age 50 · fever alongside digestive symptoms. **Protein and resistance training together.** Otherwise you gain fat, not muscle — and that's not what you asked for.
**Liquid calories.** Smoothies, milkshakes, lassi. Easier to consume when appetite is the constraint.

Whole milk, paneer, curd · nuts, seeds, nut butters · dried fruit — dates, figs, raisins · ghee and healthy oils in measured amounts · bananas, mangoes, avocado · rice, roti, potato, sweet potato · dal, rajma, chana · eggs, chicken, fish · homemade shakes and lassi

## Common myths
**"Just eat junk food."** It'll add weight — mostly fat, along with a poor lipid profile. Not the goal.
**"Protein powder alone will do it."** Without an overall calorie surplus and resistance training, it won't.
